Binoculars: the numbers explained

Updated 18 November 2026

8x42 or 10x50? The two numbers tell you most of how a pair of binoculars behaves.

In short

  • The first number is magnification, the second is objective diameter in millimetres.
  • Higher magnification gives a narrower field of view and more shake.
  • A larger objective gives a brighter image at dusk but weighs more.

Magnification

At 8x the subject looks eight times closer. 10x shows more detail at long range, but the image is harder to hold steady by hand.

Objective and brightness

Divide the objective diameter by the magnification to get the exit pupil. 42 divided by 8 gives just over 5 millimetres. A larger figure gives a brighter image at dawn and dusk.

Field of view

Field of view is given as metres of width at 1,000 metres. A wide field makes it easier to find and follow moving game and birds.

Sealing

For hunting and hiking, binoculars should be waterproof and gas-filled so they do not fog inside when the temperature changes.
