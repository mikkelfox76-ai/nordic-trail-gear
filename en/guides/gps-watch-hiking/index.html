GPS watches for hiking: what to look for

Updated 18 November 2026

A GPS watch can show the way, log the trip and warn about weather. These features matter most when you are far from people.

In short

  • Look at battery life in GPS mode, not in watch mode.
  • Buttons work better than a touchscreen with gloves and in rain.
  • The watch does not replace a map and compass.

Battery life

Manufacturers quote several figures. The one that counts on a trip is hours with GPS switched on. Check whether the watch has a saving mode for multi-day trips.

Maps and navigation

Some watches carry full maps, others only show a line to follow. On-watch maps help in unfamiliar terrain but raise the price.

Satellite systems

Watches that use several satellite systems and frequency bands hold position better in narrow valleys and dense forest. That uses more power.

Controls

Physical buttons work with mittens and a wet screen. Check that the display is readable in sunlight and that the strap fits over a jacket.
