What do you actually need to bring?

Winter packing list: what goes in the pack

A good winter trip starts on the living room floor the night before. Lay everything out, group by group, and you will see what is missing before you are standing at the door.

Updated 18 November 2026

It is the night before the trip. The skis are waxed and standing in the hallway, and on the living room floor there is a pile of clothes, bags and small things that does not look like it will fit anywhere. That is exactly right. This is how it should look.

Laying everything out before it goes in the pack is the simplest check there is. You see what you have, and you see what is missing. The list below follows the Norwegian Trekking Association's (DNT) packing list for winter trips in the mountains, sorted into five groups.

10–14 kgis what the gear usually weighs, according to DNT. That is without skis, boots, poles, food and drink. The pack itself often ends up at around eight kilos.

The whole list in one minute

Five groups on the floor

Go through one group at a time. Whatever is left on the floor at the end is what you forgot to think about.

1. What you wear

  • Wool base layers and wool socks
  • Wool sweater or fleece
  • Windproof jacket and trousers
  • Hat and neck gaiter
  • Windproof mittens with wool mittens inside
  • Ski boots and gaiters

2. The dry reserve

  • Extra hat, neck gaiter and mittens
  • Extra wool socks
  • One extra set of wool base layers
  • Thick wool sweater or down jacket
  • Rain gear if your outer layer is not waterproof

3. What you hope you never need

  • Wind sack
  • Sleeping pad
  • Warm sleeping bag
  • Shovel and probe
  • First aid kit with blister plasters and sports tape
  • Reflective vest

4. What finds the way

  • Map, compass and map case
  • Headlamp
  • Phone and power bank
  • Ski goggles

5. What keeps you going

  • Thermos
  • Food and drink for the day
  • Sunglasses and sunscreen
  • Ski wax, possibly skins
  • Multi-tool and tape
  • Matches or a lighter

Three things DNT says should always come along

The wind sack, the warm sleeping bag and the sleeping pad have a special place on the list. DNT writes that they should always be included on winter trips, because they are what gets you through a night out if something goes wrong. Read more in the story about the night you didn't plan.

An avalanche transceiver, shovel and probe belong together when you travel in avalanche terrain. DNT recommends practising with the gear before the trip.

Two sets of wool beat one thick one

DNT recommends two sets of wool base layers: one to ski in, and one kept dry until you arrive. The dry set is what brings the warmth back after a sweaty day.

An insulated jacket is good to keep within easy reach. It goes on over everything during breaks, so you do not get cold while you eat.

Bring the phone, even without coverage

DNT asks you to bring your phone even where there is no signal, because it can help rescuers find you. Bring a power bank. Cold drains batteries quickly, which we cover in the guide to power banks in the cold.

Map and compass are still what you navigate by. They do not run out of power.

Heavy at the door, just right when the weather turns

A pack filled from this list feels heavy when you lift it at home. Most of the weight is things you hope you will not use. That is the whole point. On the day the weather turns, the pack is just right.
