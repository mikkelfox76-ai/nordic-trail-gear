Winter hiking shoes: warmth, grip and fit

Updated 18 November 2026

Ordinary hiking shoes soon get cold and slippery once the snow arrives. Here is what sets a winter hiking shoe apart, and what to check before you buy.

In short

  • Insulation is measured in grams per square metre: around 200 grams for active hiking, around 400 grams if you move less or it is very cold.
  • The membrane keeps snow out, but not sweat. Wool socks and a dry spare pair do most for warmth.
  • The shoe should not squeeze. Tight shoes reduce circulation and make your feet colder.

Insulation in grams

The insulation figure is density, not weight: 200 grams means 200 grams of fill per square metre of fabric. Around 200 grams is usually right for shoes you hike actively in, because your feet produce heat themselves. Around 400 grams suits days when you move less, take long breaks or it is very cold. If you will stand still for hours, such as ice fishing or waiting on a hunting stand, even more insulation is common. Manufacturers' temperature estimates assume you are moving, so expect your feet to cool faster when you stop. A higher cuff also keeps in more warmth and keeps out more snow than a low shoe.

Moisture from inside

A waterproof membrane stops meltwater and wet snow, but it breathes poorly in the cold. If you are active, your socks will often get damp from sweat anyway. Choose wool socks, which insulate even when damp, and carry a dry spare pair. Take the insoles out in the evening so the shoe dries faster. Well-proofed leather lets more moisture out than a membrane, but needs regular care, especially along the seams.

Grip on snow and ice

Deep lugs on the sole give grip in loose snow, and a rubber compound made for cold stays softer when it freezes. On bare ice, though, no sole is good enough on its own: there, microspikes or light crampons provide the grip. Check that the shoe has a clear heel and toe cap so the spikes stay in place. If you walk in deep snow, gaiters are worth bringing, and a hook or ring at the front of the shoe makes them easier to attach.

Fit with winter socks

Try the shoes on with the thick socks you will wear, ideally in the afternoon when your feet have swollen a little. Your toes should move freely while the heel stays put. If you are between two sizes, pick the larger one and fill out with a thicker sock or an extra insole. Shoes that squeeze press out the warm air and reduce circulation.
