Power banks in the cold: how to choose

Updated 18 November 2026

Lithium batteries deliver less power as the temperature drops. Here is how to pick a power bank that lasts on a winter trip.

In short

  • Expect less power than the label promises when it is cold.
  • Keep the power bank warm, inside your jacket or sleeping bag.
  • Choose capacity by how many charges you need.

Capacity

Capacity is given in mAh. Some energy is lost in conversion, so you get less out than the number suggests. As a rule of thumb, 10,000 mAh gives about two full phone charges.

Cold

Cold batteries deliver less power but recover when they warm up. Do not recharge the power bank itself while it is freezing cold. Warm it first.

Weight

More capacity means more weight. A small power bank is often enough for a weekend, while a long trip off the grid needs more, or a solar panel as well.

Ports

USB-C with fast charging saves time. Check that the power bank supports the same charging standard as your phone and headlamp.
