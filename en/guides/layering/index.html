Layering: how to dress for the outdoors

Updated 18 November 2026

Three thin layers are warmer and more flexible than one thick one. Here is how the principle works.

In short

  • Wool next to skin keeps you warm even when damp.
  • Avoid cotton next to the body.
  • Take a layer off before you sweat, and put it back on at the break.

Base layer

The base layer should move moisture away from the skin. Wool keeps warming when damp. Cotton holds moisture and cools you down.

Mid layer

The mid layer insulates. Fleece, wool or a thin down or synthetic jacket holds the air your body has warmed.

Outer layer

The shell stops wind and water. It does not need to be warm in itself. Its job is to protect the layers underneath.

Adjust as you go

Start slightly cool. Take a layer off on the climbs, and put on a warm jacket as soon as you stop.
