Shell jacket or rain jacket? The difference explained

Updated 18 November 2026

Both keep the rain out, but they are made for different use. The choice comes down to how much you move.

In short

  • A rain jacket is for standing still or walking slowly in rain.
  • A shell jacket breathes and suits activity.
  • The water-repellent finish must be renewed for the jacket to work.

The difference

A simple rain jacket is waterproof but lets little moisture out. A shell jacket has a membrane that stops water from outside and lets vapour out from inside.

Water column and breathability

Water column is given in millimetres and tells you how much water pressure the fabric withstands. Higher is tighter. Breathability decides how fast sweat escapes. Underarm vents help a lot.

Two, two-and-a-half or three layers

Three-layer shells have the membrane bonded between face fabric and lining and take the most wear. Two-and-a-half layers are lighter and pack smaller but wear out sooner.

Care

When water stops beading on the outside, the face fabric soaks up water and the jacket breathes poorly. Wash it as the label says and renew the water-repellent finish.
