Sleeping pads and R-value: how warm do you need?

Updated 18 November 2026

The R-value tells you how well the pad insulates against the ground. Here is how to use the number.

In short

  • A higher R-value means a warmer pad.
  • R-values add up when you use two pads.
  • On snow, a foam pad under the inflatable one is good insurance.

What the R-value says

The R-value measures resistance to heat loss. Pads tested to the same standard can be compared directly across brands.

How much you need

As a rule of thumb, an R-value around 2 is enough in summer, while you should aim for 4 or more when sleeping on snow. If you sleep cold, go higher.

Types

Foam pads are cheap and cannot puncture, but are thin. Inflatable pads are the most comfortable and pack smallest. Self-inflating pads sit in between.

Winter use

Put a foam pad under the inflatable one. The R-values add up, and you still have insulation if the inflatable goes flat.
